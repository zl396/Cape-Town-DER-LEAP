KEY FINDINGS (PRIORITY)
================================================================================

🔴 CRITICAL INSIGHT: Temporal Mismatch Dominates
   - DER mitigation potential is NOT about total installed capacity
   - It's about EFFECTIVE capacity during load shedding hours with islanding capability
   - Morning load shedding (6-9am): ~30% solar CF (moderate overlap)
   - Evening load shedding (5-9pm): ~12.5% solar CF (SEVERE mismatch)

📈 Storage Transforms the Equation
   - WITHOUT storage: 72 MW evening capacity (2030)
   - WITH storage: 292 MW evening capacity (2030)
   - Improvement: 4× increase (306%)
   - By 2040: DER+storage can reduce evening Stage 4 → Stage 1
   - By 2050: DER+storage can eliminate evening Stage 4 burden entirely

🏘️ Household Resilience is Limited and Inequitable
   - Only 10.8% of households can island during load shedding
   - High-income: 52% of their households can island (68% of total islanding)
   - Low-income: 3.8% of their households can island (4% of total islanding)
   - EQUITY CONCERN: Two-tier electricity system exacerbates inequality

🎯 System-Level Impact (Stage 4, 2030 with storage)
   - Morning: 29% of Cape Town's burden covered
   - Evening: 49% of Cape Town's burden covered
   - Interpretation: Approaching significant relief, but not elimination

POLICY IMPLICATIONS (5 MAJOR RECOMMENDATIONS)
================================================================================

1. SUBSIDIZE BATTERY STORAGE
   - Storage is non-negotiable for effective load shedding mitigation
   - Mandate ≥5 kWh battery for incentive eligibility
   - Retrofit programs for existing solar systems

2. ADDRESS EQUITY CONCERNS
   - Means-tested storage subsidies: 80% for low-income, 40% for middle-income
   - Community battery storage for low-income areas
   - Prevent load shedding inequality from worsening

3. MANDATE ISLANDING-CAPABLE INVERTERS
   - Update inverter requirements to include islanding capability
   - Retrofit incentives for existing systems
   - Consumer education: grid-tied ≠ backup power

4. OPTIMIZE LOAD SHEDDING SCHEDULES
   - Innovative idea: shift load shedding to midday (11am-2pm) when solar CF is 75-90%
   - Trade-off: residential convenience vs. industrial disruption
   - Benefit: DER-rich areas could avoid load shedding entirely

5. MONITOR REVENUE EROSION
   - Prevent utility death spiral
   - See Part 2 analysis (deferred to future work)

METHODOLOGY SUMMARY
================================================================================

Data Sources:
- Eskom load shedding stage definitions (1-8)
- Cape Town DER-LEAP Bass model projections (S3 Progressive Subsidy)
- Typical solar generation curves for Cape Town
- Income group parameters (saturation, capacity, battery adoption)

Key Calculations:
1. Temporal overlap: solar CF during load shedding hours (6-9am, 5-9pm)
2. Effective capacity: total DER × temporal CF
3. Storage benefit: midday surplus shifted to evening (45% efficiency)
4. Household resilience: % with solar + battery ≥ 5 kWh
5. System impact: coverage % of Cape Town's load shedding burden (15% of national)

Assumptions:
- Solar CF: 30% morning, 12.5% evening, 85% midday
- Battery penetration: 50% of DER systems by 2030
- Storage round-trip: 45% utilization (conservative)
- Cape Town share: 15% of national load shedding
- Household load: 2 kW average
